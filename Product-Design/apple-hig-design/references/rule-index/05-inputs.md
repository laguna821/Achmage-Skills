## Action button (`action-button`)

<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-button>

### Best practices

- `action-button/best-practices/support-the-action-button-with-a` **Do:** Support the Action button with a small set of your app's most essential functions, and don't add a shortcut that merely opens the app. **Why:** The system already provides app opening, and icons, widgets and complications offer other quick ways to open it. · P purpose, simplicity · L value, platform · S define · M t/-/-/-
- `action-button/best-practices/for-each-action-you-support-write` **Do:** Label each Action button action with a short phrase of at most three words: title-style capitalization, starting with a present-tense verb, no articles or prepositions. **Why:** People read these labels in Settings when configuring the button, so brevity and consistency help. · P simplicity, familiarity · L content · S write · M t/-/-/-
- `action-button/best-practices/prefer-letting-the-system-show-people` **Do:** Rely on the system to explain how to configure the Action button; don't create content that repeats its guidance or usage tips. **Why:** The system already helps people set it up, so duplicates add redundancy. · P simplicity, familiarity · L content, flow · S write, interact · M t/-/-/-
### Platform considerations > iOS

- `action-button/platform-considerations/ios/let-people-use-your-actions-without` [iOS] **Do:** On iOS, let actions work without leaving the current context, using lightweight options like Live Activities or custom snippets rather than opening the app. **Why:** People can complete a quick action without switching context, as with the Set Timer example. · P agency, flexibility · L flow, platform · S interact · M t/-/-/-
### Platform considerations > watchOS

- `action-button/platform-considerations/watchos/consider-offering-a-secondary-function-that` [watchOS] **Do:** On watchOS, consider a simple secondary function that logically extends the primary Action button action, and think hard before adding more than one. **Why:** People often press without looking, and extra functions add mental load. · P agency, simplicity · L interaction, platform · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `action-button/platform-considerations/watchos/prefer-using-subsequent-button-presses-to` [watchOS] **Do:** On watchOS, use extra Action button presses for added functionality, not to stop the main task; offer stopping within the interface and treat pausing as separate. **Why:** Stopping and pausing differ, so stopping belongs in the interface. · P familiarity, agency · L interaction, platform · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `action-button/platform-considerations/watchos/pause-the-current-function-when-people` [watchOS] **Do:** On watchOS, pause the current function when the Action and side buttons are pressed together, except where a pause could endanger someone, such as a dive. **Why:** People expect this behavior, but pausing a dive may cause a diver to lose track of depth or time. · P familiarity, responsibility · L interaction, platform · S interact · M -/-/-/-

## Apple Pencil and Scribble (`apple-pencil-and-scribble`)

<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pencil-and-scribble>

### Best practices

- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/best-practices/support-behaviors-people-intuitively-expect-when` **Do:** Support actions people naturally try with real pens and pencils, such as writing in margins, when designing for Apple Pencil. **Why:** People's experience with marking tools shapes what they expect from Apple Pencil. · P familiarity, delight · L interaction, platform · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/best-practices/let-people-choose-when-to-switch` **Do:** Make app controls respond to Apple Pencil as well as finger, so people choose when to switch inputs. **Why:** A control that ignores Pencil seems unresponsive, as if the device were broken or low on battery. · P agency, flexibility · L interaction, inclusion · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/best-practices/let-people-make-a-mark-the` **Do:** Let marks start the moment Apple Pencil touches the screen, without a button tap or special mode first. **Why:** Using Pencil should feel like pencil on paper. · P agency, delight · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/best-practices/help-people-express-themselves-by-responding` **Do:** Use Apple Pencil's tilt, force, orientation and barrel roll to shape strokes, keeping pressure effects simple, such as varying opacity or brush size continuously. **Why:** Responding to how people hold and press expresses their intent; continuous properties feel natural for pressure. · P delight, familiarity · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/best-practices/provide-visual-feedback-to-indicate-a` **Do:** Show immediate visual response where Apple Pencil touches, and don't let it trigger actions that seem unrelated or change content elsewhere on screen. **Why:** The Pencil should look like it directly manipulates the touched content. · P familiarity, simplicity · L interaction, attention · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/best-practices/design-a-great-left-and-right` **Do:** Place controls so neither hand covers them, and let people reposition controls that could be obscured, supporting both left- and right-handed use. **Why:** Hands resting on the screen can block controls. · P flexibility, agency · L attention, inclusion · S compose · M t/-/-/-
### Hover

- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/hover/use-hover-to-help-people-predict` **Do:** Use Apple Pencil hover to preview the mark a tool will make, keeping the preview steady instead of changing it as height varies. **Why:** A height-dependent preview doesn't clarify what the mark will look like. · P familiarity, simplicity · L interaction, attention · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/hover/avoid-using-hover-to-initiate-an` **Do:** Don't trigger actions from Apple Pencil hover, especially destructive ones. **Why:** Hovering is imprecise and people could act by accident just by holding the Pencil near the screen. · P responsibility, agency · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/hover/prefer-showing-a-preview-value-thats` **Do:** Preview mid-range values for dynamic properties like opacity or flow instead of extremes. **Why:** A maximum-pressure preview could cover the work area and a minimum one could be too faint to see, making either misleading. · P craft · L attention, interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/hover/consider-using-hover-to-support-relevant` **Do:** Use hover for relevant actions near the marking spot, for example a tool-size menu after a squeeze or modifier key press. **Why:** People can choose without moving the Pencil or hands elsewhere. · P agency, simplicity · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/hover/prefer-showing-hover-previews-for-apple` **Do:** Show hover previews for Apple Pencil only, not for pointing devices, where appropriate. **Why:** Identical feedback for both devices could confuse people. · P familiarity, simplicity · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
### Double tap

- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/double-tap/respect-peoples-settings-for-the-double` **Do:** Honor the system double-tap setting for Apple Pencil when your app supports the behaviors, so people can use their preferred gesture. **Why:** People can set double-tap to toggle tools, show the color picker or do nothing. · P agency, familiarity · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/double-tap/give-people-a-way-to-specify` **Do:** If you add custom double-tap behavior, give people a control to select it and make the active mode and the custom behavior easy to find. **Why:** Not knowing the current mode leads to confusion when the app responds differently. · P familiarity, agency · L interaction, content · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/double-tap/avoid-using-the-double-tap-gesture` **Do:** Avoid double-tap for actions that change content; prefer easily undone actions and never data-losing ones. **Why:** Accidental double-taps can happen unnoticed. · P responsibility, agency · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
### Squeeze

- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/squeeze/treat-squeeze-as-a-single-quick` **Do:** Treat Apple Pencil squeeze as one quick gesture triggering a discrete action, and show the outcome promptly. **Why:** Holding or repeating squeezes with force is tiring. · P agency, simplicity · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/squeeze/if-you-use-squeeze-to-reveal` **Do:** Show squeeze-triggered UI like a contextual menu close to the Apple Pencil Pro tip. **Why:** Proximity strengthens the link between gesture and result and keeps people engaged in their task. · P delight, familiarity · L interaction, attention · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/squeeze/define-squeeze-actions-that-are-nondestructive` **Do:** Make squeeze actions nondestructive and easy to undo; don't use them where data could be lost. **Why:** People can squeeze unintentionally. · P responsibility, agency · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
### Barrel roll

- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/barrel-roll/use-barrel-roll-only-to-modify` **Do:** Use barrel roll only to adjust marking behavior, not for navigation or revealing controls. **Why:** Barrel roll is naturally tied to marking, unlike double-tap and squeeze. · P familiarity, simplicity · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
### Scribble

- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/scribble/make-text-entry-feel-fluid-and` **Do:** Make writing with Scribble flow without extra steps; if you use a custom text field, don't make people tap it first. Standard fields except password fields support it by default. **Why:** Handwriting entry should feel effortless. · P agency, familiarity · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/scribble/make-scribble-available-everywhere-people-might` **Do:** Offer Scribble wherever text entry feels natural, such as writing a new item in blank space under a list. **Why:** Pencil encourages treating the screen like paper, so writing should work in expected places. · P delight, familiarity · L interaction, platform · S interact, compose · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/scribble/avoid-distracting-people-while-they-write` **Do:** Hide autocompletion suggestions and placeholder text as writing begins in a field. **Why:** Keyboard-oriented behaviors can visually interfere with handwriting. · P simplicity, familiarity · L interaction, attention · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/scribble/while-people-are-writing-in-a` **Do:** Keep a text field fixed in place while someone writes; if it must move or resize, handle it with care. **Why:** Moving fields make people feel they lost control of where input goes. · P agency · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/scribble/prevent-autoscrolling-text-while-people-are` **Do:** Don't autoscroll text while people write or edit in a field. **Why:** People avoid writing over moving text, and scrolling during selection can select the wrong range. · P agency, familiarity · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/scribble/give-people-enough-space-to-write` **Do:** Enlarge text fields ahead of Apple Pencil writing, before it starts or during pauses, but never mid-writing. **Why:** A cramped field is uncomfortable for handwriting. · P flexibility, craft · L interaction · S compose, interact · M t/-/-/-
### Custom drawing

- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/custom-drawing/help-people-draw-on-top-of` **Do:** Disable dynamic color adjustment on a PencilKit canvas when people draw over existing content like a PDF or photo. **Why:** Automatic Dark Mode color changes could make markup hard to see over such content. · P craft · L attention, inclusion · S express, interact · M t/-/-/-
- `apple-pencil-and-scribble/custom-drawing/consider-displaying-custom-undo-and-redo` **Do:** In compact layouts, consider adding your own undo and redo buttons in a toolbar and the standard 3-finger gesture. **Why:** The tool picker lacks undo and redo there. · P flexibility, agency · L interaction, platform · S interact, adapt · M t/-/-/-

## Camera Control (`camera-control`)

<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/camera-control>

### Best practices

- `camera-control/best-practices/use-sf-symbols-to-represent-control` **Do:** For Camera Control items, choose a clear SF Symbol; custom symbols aren't supported and symbols don't reflect state. **Why:** The system only supports its own symbols, so pick one that denotes behavior. · P familiarity, simplicity · L interaction, content · S express · M -/-/-/-
- `camera-control/best-practices/keep-names-of-controls-short` **Do:** Keep Camera Control names short. **Why:** Labels follow Dynamic Type and long names could cover the viewfinder. · P simplicity, flexibility · L content, attention · S write · M -/-/-/-
- `camera-control/best-practices/include-units-or-symbols-with-slider` **Do:** Add units or symbols such as EV or % to Camera Control slider values. **Why:** Context shows people what the slider adjusts. · P simplicity, familiarity · L content · S write · M -/-/-/-
- `camera-control/best-practices/define-prominent-values-for-a-slider` **Do:** Define prominent values for a Camera Control slider, like frequent choices or even steps such as zoom increments. **Why:** The system snaps more easily to those values while sliding. · P craft, familiarity · L interaction · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `camera-control/best-practices/make-space-for-the-overlay-in` **Do:** Keep your camera UI out of Camera Control overlay areas in both orientations, with the viewfinder as large as possible. **Why:** The overlay and labels occupy that area, and your UI would collide with them. · P simplicity, craft · L attention, platform · S compose · M -/-/-/-
- `camera-control/best-practices/minimize-distractions-in-the-viewfinder` **Do:** Leave the viewfinder clear and don't duplicate sliders or toggles that the system overlay already shows. **Why:** People value a large preview with few distractions. · P simplicity · L attention · S compose · M -/-/-/-
- `camera-control/best-practices/enable-or-disable-controls-depending-on` **Do:** Enable or disable Camera Control controls based on camera mode, such as disabling video controls during photos; the set can't change at runtime. **Why:** Controls should fit the current mode, and the overlay's controls are fixed. · P familiarity, simplicity · L interaction · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `camera-control/best-practices/consider-how-to-arrange-your-controls` **Do:** Order controls with common ones in the middle and rare ones at the sides; the system remembers the last-used control. **Why:** Middle placement gives quick access to frequent controls. · P simplicity, agency · L interaction, attention · S compose · M -/-/-/-
- `camera-control/best-practices/allow-people-to-use-the-camera` **Do:** Let people launch your camera experience from anywhere through Camera Control by building a locked camera capture extension. **Why:** People can configure it to open your camera from a locked device, Home Screen or other apps. · P agency, flexibility · L flow, platform · S structure · M -/-/-/-

## Digital Crown (`digital-crown`)

<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/digital-crown>

### Apple Watch

- `digital-crown/apple-watch/anchor-your-apps-navigation-to-the` **Do:** On Apple Watch, build navigation around turning the Digital Crown, using vertical list, tab and scroll views, and back it up with touch. **Why:** Since watchOS 10, the Crown is the main way to move within and between apps. · P familiarity · L interaction, platform · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `digital-crown/apple-watch/consider-using-the-digital-crown-to` **Do:** Use the Digital Crown to inspect data where navigation isn't needed, such as stepping through times of day in World Clock. **Why:** It lets people explore values easily when no navigation is required. · P purpose, delight · L interaction, platform · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `digital-crown/apple-watch/provide-visual-feedback-in-response-to` **Do:** Show visual change when the Digital Crown turns, such as updating a picker's value, and update the UI if you track turns directly. **Why:** Without feedback, people assume turning the Crown does nothing. · P familiarity, simplicity · L interaction · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `digital-crown/apple-watch/update-your-interface-to-match-the` **Do:** Change the interface at a pace that follows how fast the Digital Crown turns, and don't update so quickly that choosing values is hard. **Why:** People expect precise control from the Crown. · P craft, familiarity · L interaction · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `digital-crown/apple-watch/use-the-default-haptic-feedback-when` **Do:** Keep the default Digital Crown haptics when suitable; turn off detents that clash with your animation, or use linear detents for tables with varied row heights. **Why:** Haptics should feel right in the app's context. · P delight, familiarity · L interaction · S interact · M -/-/-/-

## Eyes (`eyes`)

<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes>

### Best practices

- `eyes/best-practices/always-give-people-multiple-ways-to` **Do:** Support multiple ways to interact with your app, including the accessibility features people use to personalize input. **Why:** People customize how they interact with devices. · P agency, flexibility · L inclusion, interaction · S include, interact · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/best-practices/design-for-visual-comfort` **Do:** On visionOS, keep objects people need inside their field of view for visual comfort; the system initially places windows in front of them. **Why:** Comfort in view supports completing the main task. · P flexibility, craft · L attention, inclusion · S compose, adapt · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/best-practices/place-content-at-a-comfortable-viewing` **Do:** On visionOS, put content about a meter or more away if people will read or engage with it for a while; close content is only for brief use. **Why:** Comfortable distance prevents strain over time. · P flexibility, craft · L inclusion, attention · S compose · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/best-practices/prefer-using-standard-ui-components` **Do:** On visionOS, prefer standard UI components. **Why:** They respond consistently to gaze, while custom visual cues are hard to learn and remember. · P familiarity · L interaction, platform · S compose · M -/-/-/-
### Making items easy to see

- `eyes/making-items-easy-to-see/minimize-visual-distractions` **Do:** On visionOS, cut visual noise and movement, especially at the periphery, so people can keep looking at their target. **Why:** Noise hinders finding objects, and peripheral motion automatically draws the eyes away. · P simplicity · L attention · S compose · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/making-items-easy-to-see/make-it-easy-for-people-to` **Do:** On visionOS, leave at least 16 points around interactive items, or equivalent spacing, so a person can look at one without drifting to its neighbor. **Why:** Eyes make small quick adjustments, so crowded items are hard to fixate. · P flexibility, craft · L interaction, attention · S compose · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/making-items-easy-to-see/avoid-using-a-repeating-pattern-or` **Do:** On visionOS, avoid patterns or textures filling the whole field of view; use them in small areas. **Why:** Eyes can lock onto different pattern elements and perceive different depths. · P flexibility, craft · L attention, inclusion · S express · M -/-/-/-
### Encouraging interaction

- `eyes/encouraging-interaction/consider-using-subtle-visual-cues-to` **Do:** On visionOS, nudge attention to the likely target with subtle cues like central placement, gentle motion, contrast, or color or scale changes, without being harsh. **Why:** Subtle cues guide gaze without feeling flashy. · P simplicity, delight · L attention · S express, compose · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/encouraging-interaction/in-general-give-an-interactive-item` **Do:** On visionOS, give interactive items rounded shapes. **Why:** Eyes drift to corners, so rounder shapes make it easier to target the center. · P familiarity, craft · L interaction, attention · S express · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/encouraging-interaction/if-you-create-an-interactive-component` **Do:** On visionOS, give multi-element interactive components one containing shape so the whole thing highlights when either part is looked at. **Why:** The system needs a region that includes all elements to highlight. · P familiarity, craft · L interaction · S compose, interact · M -/-/-/-
### Custom hover effects

- `eyes/custom-hover-effects/prefer-using-a-custom-hover-effect` **Do:** On visionOS, reserve custom hover effects for special moments and avoid overusing them where standard ones suffice. **Why:** People know standard effects, so custom ones stand out, but too many dilute impact, distract and cause discomfort. · P simplicity, delight · L attention, interaction · S express, interact · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/custom-hover-effects/choose-the-right-delay` **Do:** On visionOS, choose when a custom hover effect appears: immediately for subtle or inviting ones, or after a short or longer delay depending on the interaction. **Why:** Timing should match how people will use the element. · P craft, familiarity · L interaction · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/custom-hover-effects/aim-to-keep-one-or-more` **Do:** On visionOS, keep at least one main view of an element unchanged during a custom hover effect. **Why:** A stable view helps people follow the change; if everything moves they get disoriented. · P familiarity, craft · L attention, interaction · S express · M -/-/-/-
- `eyes/custom-hover-effects/thoroughly-test-custom-hover-effects` **Do:** On visionOS, thoroughly test custom hover effects, ideally while wearing Apple Vision Pro. **Why:** Only testing shows if they look good and respond well without being distracting. · P craft · L interaction · S interact · M -/-/-/-

## Focus and selection (`focus-and-selection`)

<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/focus-and-selection>

### Best practices

- `focus-and-selection/best-practices/rely-on-system-provided-focus-effects` **Do:** Use the system's focus effects; make custom ones only if truly necessary. **Why:** System effects are tuned for Apple devices and give consistency and predictability. · P familiarity · L interaction, attention · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `focus-and-selection/best-practices/avoid-changing-focus-without-peoples-interaction` **Do:** Don't move focus unless people have acted, except with discrete directional input devices like a keyboard, remote or game controller. **Why:** People use focus to know where they are, and surprise shifts make them search and slow down. · P agency, familiarity · L interaction, flow · S interact · M t/d/-/-
- `focus-and-selection/best-practices/be-consistent-with-the-platform-as` **Do:** Follow platform norms for focus: on iPadOS and macOS, support focus for content elements only, while tvOS needs it more broadly. **Why:** Full keyboard access covers controls there, while tvOS depends on directional navigation. · P familiarity, flexibility · L interaction, platform · S interact, adapt · M t/d/-/-
- `focus-and-selection/best-practices/indicate-focus-using-visual-appearances-that` **Do:** Show focus with the platform's own look, such as accent-colored highlight with white text for focused list rows on iPadOS and macOS. **Why:** Consistent visuals match platform expectations. · P familiarity, craft · L interaction, attention · S express · M t/d/-/-
- `focus-and-selection/best-practices/in-general-use-a-focus-ring` **Do:** Use a focus ring for text and search fields but a highlight for lists and collections. **Why:** Highlighting whole rows makes lists and collections easier to view. · P familiarity, simplicity · L attention, interaction · S express · M t/d/-/-
### Platform considerations > iPadOS

- `focus-and-selection/platform-considerations/ipados/customize-the-halo-focus-effect-when` [iPadOS] **Do:** On iPadOS, tweak the halo focus effect when the default doesn't match, refining its contour or position, such as when a badge occludes it. **Why:** The system infers a halo from item shape, which may not fit. · P craft, familiarity · L attention, platform · S express · M t/-/-/-
- `focus-and-selection/platform-considerations/ipados/ensure-that-focus-moves-through-your` [iPadOS] **Do:** On iPadOS, make Tab key focus move through custom views in a sensible order, adjusting it if needed. **Why:** Focus follows reading order by default, but custom views may need a different path. · P familiarity, flexibility · L interaction, flow · S interact · M t/d/-/-
- `focus-and-selection/platform-considerations/ipados/adjust-the-priority-of-an-item` [iPadOS] **Do:** On iPadOS, give the most important item in a focus group higher priority so it receives focus first. **Why:** The group's primary item takes focus automatically, making the likely choice easy. · P purpose, agency · L value, interaction · S interact · M t/d/-/-
### Platform considerations > tvOS

- `focus-and-selection/platform-considerations/tvos/in-a-full-screen-experience-let` [tvOS] **Do:** On tvOS, in full-screen content let gestures act on the content rather than moving focus. **Why:** With no focus shown, people assume gestures affect the object. · P familiarity, agency · L interaction · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `focus-and-selection/platform-considerations/tvos/avoid-displaying-a-pointer` [tvOS] **Do:** On tvOS, avoid showing a pointer; use focus for menus and interface elements, and make any needed pointer highly visible. **Why:** People expect to change focus among fixed items rather than drag a tiny pointer across a huge screen. · P familiarity, simplicity · L interaction, platform · S interact · M -/-/-/-
- `focus-and-selection/platform-considerations/tvos/design-your-interface-to-accommodate-components` [tvOS] **Do:** On tvOS, plan for each focus state's look, supply sharp larger assets for focused items and ensure they don't crowd neighbors. **Why:** Focused items often scale up, so assets and spacing must hold up. · P craft, flexibility · L attention, interaction · S express, compose · M -/-/-/-

## Game controls (`game-controls`)

<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls>

### Touch controls

- `game-controls/touch-controls/determine-whether-it-makes-sense-to` **Do:** Decide whether virtual controls over game content are worthwhile; they suit games with many actions or movement control, but consider tying actions to in-game gestures and objects to cut overlap. **Why:** Direct interaction with in-game objects can be more immersive and effective than overlaid controls. · P purpose, simplicity · L value, interaction · S compose · M t/-/-/-
- `game-controls/touch-controls/place-virtual-buttons-where-theyre-easy` **Do:** Position virtual buttons within safe areas, clear of the Home indicator and Dynamic Island, with frequent ones near the thumb and outside movement and camera zones. **Why:** Buttons need to be comfortable and unobstructed to reach. · P flexibility, craft · L interaction, platform · S compose · M t/-/-/-
- `game-controls/touch-controls/make-sure-controls-are-large-enough` **Do:** Size frequently used controls at least 44x44 pt and less important ones like menus at least 28x28 pt. **Why:** Controls must be big enough for fingers. · P flexibility, craft · L interaction, inclusion · S compose · M t/d/-/-
- `game-controls/touch-controls/always-include-visible-and-tactile-press` **Do:** Give virtual controls a visible press state, such as a glow visible under the finger, plus sound and haptics. **Why:** Without visual and physical feedback a control feels unresponsive, and players need to know their press registered. · P familiarity, delight · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `game-controls/touch-controls/use-symbols-that-communicate-the-actions` **Do:** Use symbols that depict what each button does, like a weapon for attack, instead of abstract shapes or controller names such as A, X or R1. **Why:** Abstract shapes and controller names make functions harder to understand and remember. · P familiarity, simplicity · L attention, content · S express · M t/-/-/-
- `game-controls/touch-controls/show-and-hide-virtual-controls-to` **Do:** Show or hide virtual controls to match gameplay, hiding those that are unavailable or irrelevant, such as movement controls until a touch. **Why:** Hiding unneeded controls cuts clutter and helps players concentrate. · P simplicity, agency · L attention, flow · S compose, interact · M t/-/-/-
- `game-controls/touch-controls/combine-functionality-into-a-single-control` **Do:** Merge actions into one control by using gestures like double tap or touch and hold for variants, rather than requiring simultaneous or sequential button presses. **Why:** Multi-button demands are harder to perform; gestures can give variations of the same action. · P simplicity, agency · L interaction · S interact · M t/-/-/-
- `game-controls/touch-controls/map-movement-and-camera-controls-to` **Do:** Put movement on the left side and camera on the right, make input areas as large as possible, and show a thumbstick wherever the thumb lands. **Why:** Players expect this arrangement and benefit from large input areas. · P familiarity, craft · L interaction · S compose, interact · M t/-/-/-
### Physical controllers

- `game-controls/physical-controllers/support-the-platforms-default-interaction-method` **Do:** Make sure the platform's default input method works as a fallback if you support game controllers. **Why:** Controllers are optional while every device has its own default input. · P flexibility, familiarity · L interaction, inclusion · S interact, adapt · M t/-/-/-

### Gestures
